## 1. Opening: the canoes (0:00 to 0:50)
🎬 *Picture: pre-dawn beach, canoes coming in, hulls hauled up the sand, women at the smoking ovens. Sound: surf, shouting, wood on sand.* **Adjust every line below to what you actually film.**

> Before the sun is fully up on the Teshie shore, the canoes are already coming in.
>
> Men and boys haul the long wooden hulls up the sand. Women are already waiting, with fire, and racks, and baskets. By the time the city wakes up, the night's catch is on its way to being smoke, and money, and dinner.

> This is Teshie. A Ga town on the coast, east of Accra. And this morning looks very much like mornings here have for a very long time.

✅ Teshie is a Ga coastal town east of Accra. ⚠️ "Women at the ovens": fish smoking is traditionally women's work on this coast (one account); confirm on the day.

## 2. Where Teshie came from (0:50 to 2:50)
🎬 *Picture: old photographs if any, the sea, the lagoon at the western edge, elders' hands, maps. [ELDER SLOT: the founding story in their words. If you have this, it replaces paragraphs 2–4.]*

> Ask in Teshie how the town began, and you'll hear more than one answer. We'll tell you what has been published, and then we'll listen to the people who carry the story.

> According to the tradition of the Numo Nmashie family, as it has been written down, the story begins in the town of La, a little to the west. Two leaders shared the town. One, Odoi Atsem, became its chief, its Mantse. The other, Numo Nmashie, became his second-in-command, the Mankralo.

> Around the year seventeen-ten, the family tradition says, a quarrel grew between them. The king in Ga Mashie tried to settle it. But Numo Nmashie chose to leave. He walked east, to the land the Nungua chief held. He bought the land from the Nungua Mantse. And on that land, he founded Teshie.

> He did not crown himself. By this account, he appointed Nii Trebi as chief, and he kept his own place as Mankralo. He set up the first quarters of the town: Krobo, Agbawe, and Klemusum. And he entrusted the deity Lakpa to Klemusum.

⚠️ *Everything above is the Numo Nmashie family tradition as published by the Accra Street Journal (April 2026). Single detailed source: this is exactly what to confirm with the family and the council. Keep the phrase "according to the tradition".*

> A historian whose own ancestors helped found Teshie, Wendell Adjetey, tells it a little differently. He puts the founding earlier, in the late sixteen-hundreds, and he calls Teshie a garrison town, settled after fighting broke out in La.

> Two dates. Twenty or thirty years apart. Neither cancels the other. Teshie is, by every account, around three hundred years old.

✅ Two dates exist and both are published. ❓ *Ask the elders which they hold, and why.*

## 3. A town with a name and a reputation (2:50 to 4:10)
🎬 *Picture: stone, rock on the coast, the fort ruins. [ELDER SLOT: the meaning of "Teshie", in Ga, from the palace linguist.]*

> The name itself is a question. One published explanation says *te* means stone, and *shie* means under. Teshie: under the stone. Another says the name came from travellers who described the people living beneath a cave. We don't know yet. [**CONFIRM with the palace linguist before recording this paragraph.**]

❓ *Low confidence: only forum and popular sources. Do not record as fact.*

> What the records do agree on is the town's reputation. Teshie has long been called a warrior town. There is an old Ga saying: *Gonti ni ashiii see anmoo kpo.* [**ELDER: say it, translate it, tell us when it's used.**] The published story is that once, the whole Ga State stood behind the Teshie chief to turn back an attack on Ga Mashie.

⚠️ *The accolade and its story come from the Accra Street Journal. No translation is published. Use only what an elder gives you.*

> In eighteen twenty-six, at the Battle of Katamanso, Ga forces fought alongside the British against Asante. The record says Teshie's warriors were counted among the bravest.

⚠️ *The battle is ✅ documented; Teshie's specific role is one published source.*

> And on the shore, there is a ruin. In seventeen eighty-seven, the Danes built Fort Augustaborg here: the last European fort built on the Gold Coast. Britain bought it in eighteen-fifty and held it until independence in nineteen fifty-seven. Today it is crumbling. A fort the Europeans left, in a town that never left.

✅ Fort history is well documented. 🎬 *Visit with a local, not alone: the site is eroded and advice is to go accompanied.*

## 4. What Teshie makes (4:10 to 5:50)
🎬 *Picture: the Kane Kwei workshop, saws, paint, a coffin in the shape of a fish or a canoe, kpokpoi being prepared. [ELDER SLOT / CARVER SLOT: a carver explaining one coffin and the proverb behind it.]*

> Teshie is known around the world for something that surprises people the first time they hear it: coffins.

> In the nineteen-fifties, a carpenter named Seth Kane Kwei began building coffins in the shapes of the things a person loved, or lived by: a fish, a canoe, a cocoa pod. The Ga name for them is *abebuu adekai.* Receptacles of proverbs.

> There is a debate about who made the very first. One researcher argues other carpenters were making them around the same time. Everyone agrees that this workshop made them famous.

> Today it is run by his grandson, Eric Adjetey Anang. Their work has been shown in museums in Europe and America. But here, the coffins are not art. They are how a family says goodbye.

✅ Workshop, name and lineage. ⚠️ The "first" dispute: Regula Tschumi. Use "one researcher argues". ❓ *Ask the workshop: how many apprentices today? How are coffins ordered? Can visitors come?*

## 5. Homowo: the last to celebrate (5:50 to 6:50)
🎬 *Picture: white cloth, drums, kpokpoi, the sprinkling. **Only film what the council permits.** [ELDER SLOT: what Homowo means to Teshie.]*

> And then there is Homowo.

> The word means, hooting at hunger. It remembers a famine the Ga survived, and it gives thanks for what grew after. Corn is planted in May. Across much of the Ga country, a month of drumming and noise is silenced, to keep the ground quiet while it grows. [**CONFIRM whether and when this applies in Teshie.**] Then comes the feast: kpokpoi, steamed corn meal, with palm nut soup and smoked fish.

> Every Ga town keeps Homowo. Nungua starts the season. And Teshie, by the council's own account, is the last town to celebrate. Roughly thirty days of music and dance fill its streets. Traditionally, people wear white, in honour of the deity Tettey Laakpa.

✅ Homowo basics. ⚠️ "Teshie last", "thirty days", "white for Tettey Laakpa": from the Teshie council's history page and a news report. ❓ *Confirm the dates and what's open to visitors.*

## 6. Teshie today (6:50 to 7:50)
🎬 *Picture: the shoreline, a broken wall or a house near the water, a canoe heading out, the military academy gate, a school. [VOICES: a fisher on the catch; a young person on staying or leaving.]*

> Teshie today is a big town: more than a hundred and seventy thousand people, by the last published count. It trains soldiers and peacekeepers. It trains children.

> But the two things that have always fed it are under pressure.

> The fish are fewer. Across Ghana, studies put the fall in small fish, the kind the canoes chase, at somewhere between forty and eighty-five percent, depending on the years measured. One report found canoe fishers earning, on average, about seven US dollars a trip. These are national numbers, not Teshie's. [**FISHER: what was the catch when you started?**]

> And the sea is coming in. A University of Ghana study of Teshie's shoreline found the coast retreating in places by around two and a half metres a year, while other stretches gained. Researchers point to rising seas, sand mining, and a growing population. The loss of landing sites is already part of the story.

⚠️ *Population: 2012 estimate, old figure. Fishing and erosion numbers: published studies, national or shoreline-wide; keep the attribution.*

## 7. What's at stake, and what could come next (7:50 to 8:30)
🎬 *Picture: the town in the evening, a child on the beach, Homowo drums. [ELDER SLOT: what they hope for.]*

> There are plans in the air, and there are questions.

> The Ghana Tourism Authority has pledged to help make Homowo a showcase of Ghana's cultural calendar. The workshop's coffins already travel to museums. And the stories of this town (who founded it, what its name means, what its elders remember) are still, for the most part, held in a few memories.

> What would it look like for Teshie to benefit from visitors, rather than be consumed by them? What would protect the shoreline, and who should decide? And who is writing the town's story down, before it is lost? [**ELDER / YOUTH: your answers here.**]

⚠️ *The Tourism Authority pledge is real but has no published budget or results. Present it as a pledge.* ❓ *Homowo 2025 saw deadly clashes. If the council wants it in the film, use their words. Otherwise leave it out and let it shape the questions about safety.*

## Interview prompts for this film
**Elder / Numo Nmashie family:** Tell the founding of Teshie in your own words. Who came, why, and what did they bring? Which date do you hold: seventeen-ten or earlier? What are all the quarters today?
**Palace linguist:** What does *Teshie* mean, and how is it spelled in Ga?
**Elder:** Who or what is Tettey Laakpa and Lakpa, and how is that connected to La's Lakpa?
**Carver at Kane Kwei:** Choose one coffin and tell its proverb. Who orders them now?
**Fisher / smoker:** What was the catch when you started? What's different now? Will your children do this?
**Young person:** What do you want to keep, and what would you change?
**Council:** What may and may not be filmed at Homowo? What would you want visitors to know?
