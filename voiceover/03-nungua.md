# Film 3: Nungua, "Where the Season Begins"

**Length:** about 880 words of narration (~6.5 minutes spoken), in a film of about 9 minutes once you add natural sound and elder clips. **Tone:** warm and curious; this film holds a founding story that isn't settled, so the narrator invites rather than decides.
**Facts and sources:** [nungua-research.md](../research/nungua-research.md) and [present-and-future.md](../research/present-and-future.md). Tags: ✅ solid · ⚠️ attribute · ❓ confirm · 🎬 footage. See [00-series-plan.md](00-series-plan.md).

> **Honest note:** Nungua is the thinnest of the three on the history side. Two key sources (a rebuttal about Nungua's founding and the 2026 Kplejoo launch report) were unreadable. Use the history chapters as a **frame to be filled by the Nungua Traditional Council's own telling**, not as finished narration.

## 1. Opening: the sea gods first (0:00 to 0:50)
🎬 *Picture: Nungua landing beach, canoes, fishers at the water's edge, the Nshɔbulemɔ rite if permitted. Sound: surf, drums, voices. **Adjust to the footage.***

> Every year, before the corn is ripe and before the feast is cooked, the people of Nungua go to the sea.
>
> They go to ask the sea gods for peace, and for fish. In particular, one fish, tsile, for the meal the whole Ga coast will eat. It is how the year begins here.

✅ Nshɔbulemɔ is performed before Kplejoo. ⚠️ The "tsile for kpokpoi" detail is from the 2025 Nungua report (GBC). ❓ *Film only what's permitted.*

## 2. The first town (0:50 to 2:50)
🎬 *Picture: the coast road, the lagoon, the sacred grove if permitted, elders. [ELDER SLOT: Nungua's own story of where it came from. This replaces paragraphs 2–5.]*

> Nungua is one of the six old Ga towns. East of Teshie, west of Tema.

> The Nungua people say they are among the very first of the Ga to arrive on this coast, and that is why Nungua begins the Homowo season before every other town. We'll tell you what is published, and then we'll listen.

> The municipal assembly's own history says the people of Nungua came from a place called Tetetu, in Benin, with the first party of Ga. It says their high priest was Borketey Laweh. And it tells a story about him.

> In that story, Borketey Laweh was badly hurt by an enemy, after his own people did not stand beside him. In his pain he cursed the town: it would never grow. Then he walked into the sea, and the waters parted, and he was never seen again. The assembly's page says the town has since overcome the curse, and prospered.

⚠️⚠️ *All from the Krowor Municipal Assembly's history page, itself oral tradition. Tell the story as the town tells it, and ask an elder how it is told now and who may tell it.*

> But not everyone tells the founding the same way. One published reply argues that Borketey Laweh's descendants wrongly claim he founded the town, and credits a son, La Akotia, with founding the present town, joined by refugees and newcomers.

⚠️ *Seen only through a search summary of a Newsghana article, which couldn't be read. **Read the original before you record this line.** A real disagreement: show both, never rank.*

> Two stories about a founding. [**ELDER: how does the Nungua Traditional Council tell it?**]

## 3. The neighbours (2:50 to 3:40)
🎬 *Picture: the road west to Teshie, the lagoon boundary.*

> Nungua's story is tied to its neighbours'. Teshie's family tradition says that around seventeen-ten, Teshie's founder bought land from the Nungua chief, Afote Okley, and built a new town on it. [**ELDER, Nungua: how does Nungua remember this?**]

> One forum account describes a war in which La defeated Nungua, and the Kpeshie Lagoon became the line between them. [**ELDER: is this how it is told in Nungua?**]

> And when a new Ga Mantse is installed, reports say, part of the rites includes purification in Nungua. [**ELDER: why Nungua?**]

⚠️⚠️ *Each is single-source. The Teshie land sale: Accra Street Journal. The war: a Ga-Dangme mailing list (Low). The purification: news reports of the current Ga Mantse's rites. Ask first, narrate after.*

## 4. Kplejoo: three months of customs (3:40 to 5:00)
🎬 *Picture: the Gborbu grove if permitted, spring water carried in pots, the royal mausoleum, the sprinkling of kpokpoi, dancers, the paramount chief. **Only film what the council permits.** [ELDER SLOT: what Kplejoo is, step by step.]*

> The festival that follows is called Kplejoo. The municipal assembly describes it as a feast held on the first Sunday in June. But in practice, it is much longer. For twenty twenty-six, the town announced twenty-two customary events across about three months.

> There is a rite of carrying spring water to the sacred Gborbu grove. There are the stools to be cleansed, and prayers for food and fish. There is the sprinkling of kpokpoi at the royal mausoleum, in honour of the chiefs who are buried there.

> The priest called the Gborbu Wulomo works alongside the Nungua chief, the Mankralo, and others, to receive the holy corn that will become the Homowo feast.

> And only then does the Ga coast's Homowo season begin, town by town. Nungua first. Teshie, last.

✅ Kplejoo precedes Homowo; the 22 events and the Gborbu rites are from 2026 news reports (⚠️ the spelling varies: Kplejoo / Kpledzoo / Kpledjoo: **ask the council which they prefer**). ❓ *The Jenten Nishwamo rite: ask what it means.*

## 5. Nungua today (5:00 to 6:40)
🎬 *Picture: Nungua landing beach, big canoes, the busy road, the Regional Maritime University, young people, a market. [VOICES: a fisher; a young person; the municipal officer if willing.]*

> Nungua today is a busy, crowded town, part of the Krowor municipality, estimated by the assembly at about a hundred and forty thousand people. It hosts the Regional Maritime University, among other colleges.

⚠️ *The assembly's budget estimate (2021 census basis).*

> The fishers here face the same trouble as the whole coast: fewer fish than before. Across Ghana, studies put the fall in small fish at somewhere between forty and eighty-five percent, depending on the years measured.

> At the 2025 sea rites, the municipal chief executive told the fishermen that most of the big canoes now prefer to go to Tema. Premix fuel was still short, and outboard motors were not yet available.

⚠️ *GBC, 28 June 2025: the MCE's words. National fishing figures: published studies. Ask Nungua's fishers: "what is your catch now? why do the big canoes go to Tema?"*

> And the sea itself is coming closer. A climate envoy has said that Nungua, Teshie, La and Osu are all losing homes, buildings and some schools to the tides.

⚠️ *MyJoyOnline, April 2026.*

## 6. What's being planned (6:40 to 7:50)
🎬 *Picture: the landing beach; the Borteyman site where the factory will go; trucks on the motorway; young people. [VOICES: the stool's representative; a young person; a fisher.]*

> There are plans on the table, and they are worth knowing.

> The municipal assembly has put a wharf at Nungua's landing beach into its Medium Term Development Plan. A proper landing, so the big canoes might stay. As of now, it is a plan. There is no published cost or date.

> And in October, the Nungua Traditional Stool Authority announced a partnership with a Ghanaian company, WT Plastics, to build a plastics factory on stool land at Borteyman, near the motorway. The stated numbers: four hundred direct jobs, with priority for people of Nungua, and more than a thousand indirect. The chief of Nungua Sangonaa, Nii Sango, called it a step toward answering youth unemployment.

⚠️⚠️ *The wharf: a plan (GBC, June 2025). The factory: one outlet (MyJoyOnline, 14 Oct 2026): no investment amount, completion date or output stated. Ask the stool and the company, and check for updates the week you record. "1,400" is 400 + 1,000+, so don't say 1,400 jobs as one figure.*

## 7. Possibilities (7:50 to 8:30)
🎬 *Picture: the season's first corn, children at the Kplejoo, young apprentices, the coast at dusk. [ELDER / YOUTH / FISHER: what do you want for Nungua?]*

> Plans like these can fix things, or they can miss the point. A wharf means little if the fish aren't there. A factory means little if the jobs don't reach the young people who live nearby.

> So the questions that matter are the ones the plans don't answer. Who will fish in ten years? What would a visitor to Nungua see, and learn, and pay for, that the town itself would be proud of? Kplejoo already draws a crowd; who benefits?

> The Tourism Authority has pledged to help make Homowo a showpiece of Ghana's culture. For Nungua, which begins it, what would that look like? [**ELDER / COUNCIL: your answer.**]

⚠️ *The Tourism Authority pledge: GNA, May 2025: no budget or results published.*

## 8. Closing, and the circle (8:30 to 9:00)
> It starts here, by the sea, with a rite for the gods and a fish for the feast. And it ends at the last town, Teshie, where we began. Three towns, one coast, one season.

🎬 *Picture: the beach at first light, the camera pulling back to the whole coast. End card: "Three towns. One coast. Rediscover Ghana."*

## Interview prompts for this film
**Nungua Traditional Council / elders:** Tell Nungua's founding. Where did the people come from? Who is Borketey Laweh to you? How do you answer the story that La Akotia founded the town? What does "Nungua" mean? Why is the Ga Mantse purified in Nungua? How did Nungua come to sell land to Teshie's founder?
**Gborbu Wulomo (if willing):** What is Kplejoo, step by step? What may be filmed?
**Fishers:** What's the catch now vs. when you began? Why do the big canoes go to Tema? Would a wharf change that?
**Stool Authority / WT Plastics:** How many jobs, by when, and how will indigenes be prioritised? What is made there?
**Young people:** Would you work at a factory? What would you want your town to offer?
**Paa Joe's former apprentices:** What was the Nungua workshop like in the 1990s?
