# Film 2: La, "The Town That Looks at the Sea"

**Length:** about 880 words of narration (~6.5 minutes spoken), in a film of about 9 minutes once you add natural sound and elder clips. **Tone:** warm but steady; this film holds a live disagreement, so the narrator stays level and lets every side speak.
**Facts and sources:** [la-research.md](../research/la-research.md) and [present-and-future.md](../research/present-and-future.md). Tags: ✅ solid · ⚠️ attribute · ❓ confirm · 🎬 footage. See [00-series-plan.md](00-series-plan.md).

> **Before recording:** the La beach story broke in October 2026 and is still moving. Check the news the week you record, and **interview the La Traditional Council, SSNIT or the hotels, and the traders**, so no one is spoken for. Party politics stays out of the film.

## 1. Opening: the beach (0:00 to 0:50)
🎬 *Picture: La Pleasure Beach (as it is now: cleared ground, remaining structures, the sea), people walking, the hotels in the distance. Sound: waves, wind. **Adjust to the footage.***

> If you've been to Accra, there's a good chance you've been to this beach.
>
> Labadi. La Pleasure Beach. On a weekend, one of the busiest stretches of sand in the country. Music. Horses. Families. Vendors.

> But the beach is only the front door. Behind it, there's a town older than the hotels, older than the road, older than the country: La.

✅ La Pleasure Beach is among Accra's busiest beaches, especially on weekends. ❓ *Horses/music: one source describes them in the past tense, so say it only if you film it.*

## 2. A town older than the map (0:50 to 2:40)
🎬 *Picture: old maps (the 1629 Dutch map if you can license it), the hill of Ladoku inland, the shoreline, elders. [ELDER SLOT: where La's people say they came from, and the story of Ladoku.]*

> La is one of the six old Ga towns of this coast. Alongside Ga Mashie, Osu, Teshie, Nungua, and Tema. Each had its own stool, its own chief, its own gods.

> The people of La trace their roots to the east: to Benin, and to Bonny, in what is now Nigeria. They say they travelled inland first, and settled on a hill called Ladoku, in the Shai Hills. Then, they came down to the sea.

> We know they were here early. A Dutch map from sixteen twenty-nine shows La already standing on the coast, between Ga Mashie and Nungua. [**ELDER: how do you tell the story of leaving Ladoku?**]

✅ The 1629 Dutch map is documentary (via published Ga histories). ⚠️ The migration route is oral tradition and popular histories; say "they say" or "tradition holds". ❓ *Ask the elders what "La" means; the "Bone/Benin" explanation is low confidence.*

> At the centre of La's life is a god: Lakpa. A study of La's religion, published in nineteen sixty-seven, says the town's chief was also the priest of this god. A chief who was also a priest. In La, the throne and the shrine were bound together.

⚠️ *From a 1967 study (title and summary only, full paper not read).* ❓ *In Teshie, the Lakpa deity is held by one quarter. Ask both towns' elders how the two Lakpa are related.*

## 3. The neighbours (2:40 to 3:30)
🎬 *Picture: the road east toward Teshie; the lagoon boundary if reachable.*

> La's story is tangled up with its neighbours'.

> Teshie's own family tradition says that Teshie's founder, Numo Nmashie, was a leader in La who left after a quarrel around seventeen-ten. La, then, is where that story begins.

> And a published account says La once claimed authority from Labadi all the way to Teshie. Teshie's chiefs reject that. In twenty-eleven, a committee of the National House of Chiefs found that La, Teshie, Osu, Nungua and Tema owe no allegiance to the Ga Paramount Stool. A reminder that on this coast, each town is its own nation.

⚠️⚠️ *Contested. The La claim (Accra Street Journal) and Teshie's rejection are both published. Say each as a claim. [ELDER SLOT, La: how does La see its relationship to Teshie?]*

## 4. Homowo, and the sea (3:30 to 4:40)
🎬 *Picture: the fishing rite if permitted, canoes, the sprinkling of kpokpoi. **Only film what the council permits.** [ELDER SLOT: what the Nshɔbulemɔ is, in the priest's words.]*

> Every year, La keeps Homowo, the Ga festival of hooting at hunger.

> Before the feast, there is a rite for the sea. It is called Nshɔbulemɔ. Fishermen go out to calm the sea gods, to ask for a good harvest, for fish, for safe return. The people of La, and the visitors who come to watch, follow them to the water.

> At the centre of the festival, the senior priest of La, the Kpa Wulomo, leads the sprinkling of kpokpoi, the corn feast, to thank the ancestors. For several years, La's festival was silent, after the death of its long-reigning chief, Nii Kpobi Tettey Tsuru the Third. It returned in twenty twenty-two.

⚠️ *Nshɔbulemɔ and the Kpa Wulomo: published news reports. ❓ The dates of the La chief's death and the festival's pause aren't confirmed: **verify with the La Traditional Council before recording this paragraph.***

## 5. La today: a beach cleared (4:40 to 6:40)
🎬 *Picture: the beachfront as it stands, traders, hotels, the council hall, a vendor. [VOICES: a trader; a council spokesperson; SSNIT/hotel if they agree to speak.]*

> On the second of October, twenty twenty-six, in the early hours, security forces moved onto La Pleasure Beach. Over that day, structures along the beachfront, and at the neighbouring Polo Beach Club, were demolished.

✅ *Date and the demolition are reported by several outlets. Say "security forces". Reports differ on who supervised.*

> The beach, and the hotels beside it, are owned by SSNIT, the national pension trust. SSNIT says it wants to build an organised leisure and tourism destination there, with restaurants, lounges, water sports, and wellness. It says the beach will stay open to the public. And it says work will begin after talking with the community.

⚠️ *SSNIT's stated plan (MyJoyOnline, 12 Oct 2026). It is a statement of intent.*

> The La Traditional Council says it was never formally told before the demolition. It calls itself the traditional landowner, and asked for a pause until proper consultation. A community lawyer says the land belongs to the people of La, and that legal action has begun. The Member of Parliament says she was told the land belongs to the hotel.

⚠️ *All are claims by the named parties. The ownership is disputed, and no court ruling has been reported. **Don't say who is right.***

> The Ghana Tourism Federation says eighty-five businesses were demolished, and about four thousand jobs, many held by women, are at risk. [**TRADER: what did you lose? How long were you there?**]

⚠️ *The Federation's figures, not independently verified.*

> Reports since suggest the plans have been put on hold pending talks. We don't yet know how this will end. [**UPDATE the week you record: what is the status?**]

❓ *The "on hold" headlines could not be read in full. Confirm before saying it.*

## 6. The sea, and a shrine (6:40 to 7:30)
🎬 *Picture: waves at the shoreline, the shrine area if the priest agrees. [VOICE: Togbe Akpasso, the priest quoted in MyJoyOnline, if he'll speak.]*

> Meanwhile, another pressure is quieter, and it never stops.

> In April, a traditional priest at Labadi told a reporter that the sea is eating the land around a shrine he built more than thirty years ago. Every month, he said, it comes closer. "Without government help, we cannot do anything."

> A climate envoy has said that La, Teshie, Nungua and Osu are all losing homes, and some schools, to high tides.

⚠️ *Both from MyJoyOnline, April 2026. Quote the priest in his own voice if possible.*

## 7. Possibilities (7:30 to 8:20)
🎬 *Picture: the beach at dusk, children, the council hall, the shoreline. [ELDER / COUNCIL / TRADER / SSNIT: what a good outcome looks like.]*

> There are different pictures of La's future. They don't have to cancel each other out.

> One is a destination with more visitors, more shops, more jobs, but where the people who built the beach economy have a place in it. Traders have asked for first right of refusal on any new space. The council has asked that vendors be given space, and that those who lost be compensated.

> Another is a beach that stays a beach for La: a place for the Homowo fishing rite, for fishermen to land their catch, for families on a Sunday. The Ga Mantse has said that Greater Accra's beaches, forts and castles could earn their keep, with proper care.

> And beneath both, there's the question of the shoreline itself. What can be built where the sea is already taking the land?

> [**Who decides? Who benefits? Who has not been asked?**]

⚠️ *The traders' and council's requests are on record. The Ga Mantse's remark was made to the Tourism Authority in 2025. Present each as a view.*

## Interview prompts for this film
**La Traditional Council:** What does "La" mean? Who is the Mantse and who holds the offices? What is Lakpa, and how does it connect to Teshie's Lakpa? What may and may not be filmed? What do you want from any redevelopment?
**Fishers / Nshɔbulemɔ participants:** Why do you perform the rite? What do you ask for?
**Traders:** How long did you operate? What did you lose? What would you want from a redevelopment?
**SSNIT or hotel representatives:** What are the plans, the timeline, and how will La be involved?
**Togbe Akpasso (priest):** What was the shrine like when you built it? What do you want to happen?
**Youth:** What would you do with the beach?
