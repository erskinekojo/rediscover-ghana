# The Ga Coast: voiceover series plan

Three films, one per community: **Teshie, La, Nungua.** Each follows the same arc so they feel like a series, and each can stand alone: **the past → the town today → what's at stake → the possibilities.**

## How to use these scripts

**Every paragraph carries a tag. Read it before you record.**

| Tag | Meaning | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ **SOLID** | Several sources agree, or it is well documented | Safe to record as written |
| ⚠️ **ATTRIBUTE** | One published source, or it's a tradition or a claim | Record with the attribution built into the sentence. Don't drop it |
| ❓ **CONFIRM** | Needs an elder, the council or the shoot | Don't record yet. Replace with the interview, or fill the bracket |
| 🎬 **FOOTAGE** | Describes images we haven't shot yet | Rewrite to match what you actually film |

**Rules we're keeping, from the project brief:**
1. Never invent history, names, quotes or numbers. If it isn't in a source, it stays a `[bracket]`.
2. Different versions are shown side by side, never ranked. Oral tradition and written history are labelled as what they are.
3. Contested things (who owns land, who is chief, who founded what, violence at festivals) get every party's voice, and the narrator does not pick a side.
4. Recorded elders' words replace the narrator. The script has **[ELDER SLOTS]** where the strongest ones should go. An elder saying it beats us saying it.

## Format and timing
- Read at a calm documentary pace: about **130–140 words a minute.** Each script is roughly **900–1,000 words (6.5–7.5 minutes spoken)**, and a **9-minute film** once natural sound and elder clips fill the gaps; the chapter timecodes assume that. They're written to be trimmed: every chapter works alone, so a 3-minute version is Opening + Today + Possibilities.
- Each script is split into chapters with a rough time, narration, a **picture** note (what's on screen) and a **sound** note.
- **Natural sound** (surf, canoe hulls, kpokpoi pots, drums) does a lot of the work. Leave gaps in the narration for it.

## Shoot-day checklist (shared)
- **Consent first:** the traditional councils (Teshie, La, Nungua) before filming rituals, shrines, elders or festivals. Ask what must not be filmed. A signed or recorded consent for every person who speaks.
- **Pronunciation pass:** before recording, have a Ga speaker (ideally an elder from each town) say every Ga word and name on the list below, on camera, and use *their* pronunciation. Don't guess.
- **Update the news items the week you record:** the La beach story and the Nungua factory/wharf plans are moving.

### Ga words and names to pronounce correctly
Homowo · kpokpoi · Kplejoo / Kpledzoo · Nshɔbulemɔ · Mantse · Mankralo · Wulomo · Kpa Wulomo · Gborbu · abebuu adekai · Lakpa · Ladoku · Teshie · Numo Nmashie · Odoi Atsem · Nii Trebi · Afote Okley · Borketey Laweh · La Akotia · Katamanso · Ga Mashie · Gbetsoolo · Akomfra · Odaifio Welentsi · "Gonti ni ashiii see anmoo kpo"

## The four questions every film asks
1. **Where did we come from?** Told by those who carry it, and by the written record, side by side.
2. **What do people actually do here?** The craft, the work, the festival.
3. **What's under pressure now?** The sea, the fish, the land, the jobs.
4. **What could be next?** Not predictions: possibilities, with who is proposing them, and who hasn't been asked yet.

## The thread between the three films
- **Teshie** left **La**, by Teshie's family tradition (c. 1710), and bought land from **Nungua's** chief. The three towns are one story told from three doors.
- **Homowo runs on a relay:** Nungua begins, La and others follow, **Teshie is last.** Use this as the end of each film, pointing to the next town.
- The same sea is eating all three shorelines.
- The **Lakpa** deity appears in both Teshie and La. Ask elders in both towns how that connects.
