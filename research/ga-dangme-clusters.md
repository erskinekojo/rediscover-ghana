# The Ga-Dangme peoples, clustered (research draft, November 2026)

**Purpose:** a map of the whole Ga-Dangme (Ga-Adangbe) world, grouped into clusters of communities that sit close together or share kinship, so the documentary and the app can cover them in manageable groups. Built from published sources found online; **nothing is final until the traditional councils confirm it.** Confidence levels as in [teshie-research.md](teshie-research.md).

**Honest note:** this is a **broad survey, not a deep one.** Several sources were blogs or scraped wiki mirrors (one contained nonsense text and was discarded), and sources disagree about group boundaries. Boundaries below are **starting points to check, not settled lines.**

---

## 1. The big picture
- **Ga-Dangme** is two closely related languages, **Ga** and **Dangme**, partly mutually intelligible; Dangme speakers more often understand Ga than the reverse. Scholars (M. E. Kropp Dakubu) are the best source. [1][2] High.
- **Ga:** traditionally **six independent towns**, west to east: **Ga Mashie, Osu, La, Teshie, Nungua, Tema**. Each its own stool and chief. Some sources say seven; **ask which.** [3] High–Medium.
- **Dangme:** about **seven groups: Krobo, Ada, Prampram, Shai, Ningo, Osudoku, Kpone.** [4] Medium. **Kpone's placement varies** (some sources list it Dangme, others omit it). *Ask.*
- Ga-Dangme peoples occupy about **70% of the Greater Accra Region and 15% of the Eastern Region**. [5] Medium.
- **Migration:** Dangme tradition has the people travelling together and splitting at a place called **Lɔlɔvɔ** (linked to the Tagologo Plains), then going separate ways. **Oral accounts conflict** on earlier origins. Kropp Dakubu: in-migration "probably complete by CE 1400", while the Ga language reached the coast "probably not much before the seventeenth century." [6] Medium. *Oral tradition and scholarship should be shown side by side.*
- **Kinship across the line:** Teshie's early settlers are described as later joined by families from **La and Prampram**, Fanti-land fishermen and Ga Mashie families. [7] Low–Medium. *Proof that the Ga and Dangme are intertwined.*

## 2. The clusters

### Cluster 1: The old core. Ga Mashie (Jamestown + Ussher Town) and Osu
**Where:** the western Accra coast; Ga Mashie is "Old Accra". **Why group them:** the oldest settlement; the Ga Mantse's stool; forts and castles; urban, dense, historic.
- Ga Mashie is called **Old Accra**, made of **Ussher Town and James Town**, with a **well-organised trading system by 1557**. [3][8] Medium–High.
- **Osu** is the town of **Christiansborg Castle**; Danish, Dutch and English forts shaped Osu and Accra (Christiansborg 1661, Fort James 1672–73). [9] Medium–High (see [la-research.md](la-research.md)).
- **Contested:** the Ga Mashie throne is disputed in the courts; the Supreme Court affirmed King Tackie Teiko Tsuru II in July 2025. [10] *Not for the film unless interviewees raise it; every party's voice if so.*
- **Homowo:** sources say Homowo has its **climax in Ga Mashie**, which marks it a little earlier than La. [11]
- **Researched:** see [cluster-1-ga-mashie-osu.md](cluster-1-ga-mashie-osu.md) (seven quarters, forts, Homowo sequence, Twin Festival, Osu Castle, disputed stools).

### Cluster 2: The eastern shore. La, Teshie and Nungua
**Where:** the coast east of Osu, in sequence La → Teshie → Nungua. **Why group them:** kinship and land are tangled together.
- **La → Teshie:** by the Numo Nmashie family tradition, Teshie was founded c. 1710 by a leader from La. [7][12]
- **Nungua → Teshie:** by the same tradition, the founder bought land from the Nungua chief. [12]
- **La vs Nungua:** one forum account describes a war La won, with the Kpeshie Lagoon as the line. [13] Low.
- **A claim Teshie disputes:** the La Mantse's authority reaching from Labadi to Teshie. [14]
- **Already researched:** [teshie-research.md](teshie-research.md), [la-research.md](la-research.md), [nungua-research.md](nungua-research.md); scripts in `voiceover/`.

### Cluster 3: The harbour. Tema, Tema Manhean and Kpone
**Where:** east of Nungua, around Tema's harbour. **Why group them:** one shared event, the **1950s land acquisition** and relocation.
- About **63 square miles** were acquired in **1952** from the traditional authorities of **Tema, Nungua and Kpone**. Residents of the old fishing settlement (Harbour-Meridian area) were moved to **Tema Manhean (Tema New Town)**. [15] Medium (GNA, Oct 2026).
- **Dates conflict:** one account says resettlement in **1959**, another **1961**, and a third says the site was chosen in **1951** with the harbour built **1954–1962**. [15][16] **Check before using any date.**
- Fry & Drew were engaged; they **mapped the old village's social structure first** so the new settlement kept its identity. [16] Medium.
- **Recent disputes:** the Tema Traditional Council demolished structures in parts of Manhean; residents say **more than 1,000** were displaced, and the council said it would not compensate some residents. [17] **Contested; every party's voice.**
- **To research:** Tema's own origin story and festival, Kpone's status, what "Tema Newtown" was before.

### Cluster 4: The eastern Dangme coast. Prampram and Ningo
**Where:** east of Tema along the coast. **Why group them:** two neighbouring Dangme coastal traditional areas.
- **Prampram Traditional Area** (seat at Prampram) and **Ningo Traditional Area** (seat at Ningo). [4] Medium.
- **Ningo** tradition: after the split they "headed southward to the coast". A ModernGhana history of the Ningo people exists. [6][18] Low–Medium (a news-site article).
- **Kinship thread:** Prampram families are said to have joined Teshie. [7]
- **To research:** both towns' origin stories, festivals (Homowo is celebrated in Prampram and Ningo per some sources, [11]), fishing, salt.

### Cluster 5: The far east. Ada
**Where:** the Volta river mouth, east of the others. **Why separate:** farthest away; its own identity and festivals.
- The Ada are described as moving to the east coast after the split. [6] Low–Medium.
- Conflicting origin accounts (from the Western Sudan; the Nuer clan; Togo plateau) are **oral or popular and contradict each other**. [6]
- **To research:** Ada's own history, Asafotufiam festival, the Volta estuary.

### Cluster 6: The inland Dangme. Shai, Osudoku and Krobo
**Where:** north and inland: the Shai Hills (Dodowa), Osudoku, and **Krobo Mountain** (Yilo and Manya Krobo, paramount chief the **Konor**). **Why group them:** hills and mountains, not the sea; the Krobo are the best known.
- The Shai, Prampram and Kpone are described as going inland after the split; **the Osudoku ascended Osudoku mountain**; **Krobo settled at Krobo Mountain**, the Krobo's spiritual and physical home. [6] Medium.
- **Krobo** have two main divisions, **Yilo and Manya**. [19] High–Medium.
- **Dipo** (the Krobo puberty rite) belongs here, **not to the Ga coast**. Use it in Dangme context only.
- **Ga link:** La's tradition says its people came via **Ladoku in the Shai Hills**, which ties the Shai area to La's origin story. [see la-research.md] *A great cross-cluster thread to ask about.*

### Cluster 7: The Ga hinterland and west. (needs research)
**Where:** inland and west of Accra: Ga East (Abokobi), Ga West (Amasaman), Ga South (Ngleshie Amanfro, Bortianor), Weija, Kokrobite, Chorkor, Mamprobi, Dome.
- District capitals: Abokobi, Amasaman, Ngleshie Amanfro, Weija. [20] Medium. **These are modern district seats, not necessarily the old traditional centres.**
- **Homowo procession route:** one source says Kokrobite and Weija are on the route, which exits the region at Weija and continues into the Central Region. [20] Low.
- Little reliable history found. *Chorkor, Mamprobi, Dome and others need dedicated research and local guidance. Don't assume they are Ga "towns" in the same sense as the six.*

## 3. Cross-cluster threads (for the documentary)
- **The sea** ties Clusters 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 (all coastal).
- **Homowo** crosses 1–4 at least; **dates and order are not fixed** (set by the Ga Mantse's directive or the Dantu priest). [11]
- **Ladoku / Shai Hills** links La (Cluster 2) to the inland Dangme (Cluster 6).
- **The land** story links Teshie, Nungua, Tema and Kpone (Clusters 2 and 3).
- **Language:** Ga and Dangme meet in Teshie's own settlers.

## 4. A correction to flag
Earlier scripts say **"Nungua starts the season, Teshie is last."** Sources here say Homowo has its **climax in Ga Mashie, which marks it a little earlier than La**, and that **dates vary by town and year**. Nungua's **Kplejoo** precedes Homowo in the news reports, and Teshie being last is the Teshie council's own account. **Both may be true (Kplejoo opens the season; each town then sets its own Homowo date), but "a relay with Nungua first and Teshie last" is a simplification until the councils confirm it.** I've noted this in the voiceover plan.

## 5. How to use clusters
- **Film and research by cluster:** one base, one set of consent conversations per cluster. A cluster's elders will point to each other.
- **Depth:** Clusters 1–3 get full chapters; Clusters 4–6 get a chapter each; Cluster 7 starts with listening.
- **App:** group the map pins by cluster; colours can still show status.
