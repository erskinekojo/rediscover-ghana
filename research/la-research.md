# La (Labadi): research dossier (draft, November 2026)

**Purpose:** a starting point for the site and the shoot, built only from published sources found online. Nothing here is final until checked with the La Traditional Council, elders and the people of La. Confidence levels are as in [teshie-research.md](teshie-research.md): **High** (several sources agree or well documented), **Medium** (one solid source), **Low** (single popular source or sources conflict; treat as a question to ask).

**Research depth:** lighter than Teshie. The two best-looking historical sources were an academic paper on the Lakpa deity (found but not readable here) and the La Traditional Council's own materials (not found). Both are leads for the shoot.

---

## 1. The basics
| Fact | Confidence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Coastal Ga town, also called **Labadi** or **La**, in **La Dade-Kotopon Municipal District**, Greater Accra | High | [1] |
| Lies between **Osu** (west) and **Teshie** (east) on the coast, about 7 km by road from the airport | High | [1] |
| One of the six historically independent Ga towns (Ga Mashie, Osu, La, Teshie, Nungua, Tema) | High | [2], [3] |
| **La Pleasure Beach (Labadi Beach)** is described as the busiest beach on Ghana's coast | Medium | [4], [5] |
| Notable people: **Amon Kotei** (1915–2011), designer of Ghana's coat of arms | Medium | [1] |
| La was elevated to paramount status in 2011 (alongside Nungua and James Town) | Medium | [6] |

## 2. Where La's people came from
- **Migration story:** La's people trace their origin to **Benin / Bonny** (Nigeria), part of the broader Ga migration. One source says Teshie's people and La's people both claim Bonny origins [3][7]. Medium.
- **Ladoku:** La's earlier settlement was on a hill called **Ladoku** (Shai Hills area). One argument is that the people left Ladoku **before 1600** [7]. The Accra Street Journal adds stops at the Adjangote Hills and the Nsaki River area before the coast [6] (a popular source, so Low–Medium).
- **On the map in 1629:** La appears on a **Dutch map of 1629** as a coastal settlement between Ga Mashie and Nungua [7]. This gives a documentary anchor: La existed on this coast by then. **Medium–High.**
- **Name:** one older source suggests "La" derives from "Bone", a corruption of Benin. **Low.** *Ask the elders: what does "La" mean?*

## 3. The Lakpa deity and the chief as priest
- La's **principal deity is Lakpa**. A 1967 academic study, "The Lakpa: Principal Deity of Labadi" (Institute of African Studies Research Review) [8], says the chief of La was also the priest of the god. This matters: La's chieftaincy is bound up with a deity, not only a political office. **Medium** (title and one-line finding only; full paper not read).
- Teshie also holds a **Lakpa** deity (in the Klemusum quarter, by the Teshie family tradition). *A great shoot question: how are the Lakpa of La and the Lakpa of Teshie connected?* **Low** until an elder explains it.

## 4. Leadership and the Ga State question
- **La Mantse:** a long-reigning La Mantse, **Nii Kpobi Tettey Tsuru III**, is reported to have reigned about 30 years and died aged 89; former President Mahama paid last respects [9]. **Low on dates**: one report says the La Homowo was not celebrated again until 2022 after his death. *Verify the date and the current La Mantse with the La Traditional Council.*
- **The La Mantse's authority:** the Accra Street Journal says the La Mantse traditionally claims authority from Labadi **to Teshie** [6]. That is a claim Teshie's own accounts dispute (they say Teshie is independent, and the 2011 House of Chiefs finding says Teshie, La, Osu, Nungua and Tema hold no allegiance to the Ga Paramount Stool [10]). **Contested. Show both sides; don't resolve it on the site.**
- **Ga Mantse dispute (context only):** the Ga Mashie throne is disputed in the courts; in July 2025 the Supreme Court affirmed King Tackie Teiko Tsuru II. This is **not La's stool**, but you'll hear it mentioned. Keep out of the La film unless interviewees raise it [11].

## 5. Texture

### Homowo in La
- La holds its own Homowo, launched each year by the **La Traditional Council** [12]. The **La Kpa Wulomo** (a senior priest) led the sprinkling of kpokpoi when the festival returned in 2022 after the mourning period [9].
- A fishing rite, **La Nshɔbulemɔ**, is one of the activities that herald Homowo. It involves the people, tourists and spectators, and is described as meant to calm the sea gods for a good harvest [12][13]. **Medium.** *Great film material: ask when it happens and what may be filmed.*
- The festival's **night-time ground finale in 2023 was at "New Lakpana"** [12]. Medium.
- Hotels (e.g. Labadi Beach Hotel) sponsor the festival, and the 2024 launch was held there [12]. A reminder that La is a very urban, hotel-lined coast.
- **Dates:** the Ga Traditional Council announces the drumming ban each year; whether La is covered varies, so check yearly [14].

### The beach
- **La Pleasure Beach** is the best-known beach in Accra. Visitors pay an **entrance fee** (one visitor reported about GH₵20 on a weekday morning) and it is **very crowded at weekends and on holidays** [4][5]. **Low** on prices.
- **Update: this is now confirmed.** Structures at La Pleasure Beach were demolished on **2 October 2026**, and the land and redevelopment are now disputed between SSNIT (which owns the neighbouring hotels), the La Traditional Council, the MP and traders. See [present-and-future.md](present-and-future.md), section C, for the full account with sources. It is a live, sensitive story, so check for updates the week you record.
- The beach is also described as a site for **traditional celebrations**, not only leisure [5].

### Food, craft, fishing
- Nothing reliable found about a dish specific to La. *Ask locals.*
- Fantasy coffins are used at funerals in Teshie, Nungua and **La** [15]. No specific La workshop found.
- La is a Ga fishing community on the same coast as Teshie, but I found no La-specific fishing history.

## 6. What the written record says
- **1629 Dutch map** shows La as a coastal settlement [7].
- Danish, Dutch and English forts shaped Osu and Accra to La's west (Christiansborg 1661, Fort James 1672–73) [6]. La itself had no fort.
- The La Mantse was recognised as a **paramount chief in 2011** [10].
- Residential history: parts of La known today as **Otswe** and **South La Estates** are described as having been mostly European-occupied until independence [16]. **Low** (real-estate blog).

## 7. Getting there
- La is on the main coastal road between Osu and Teshie, so trotros and taxis heading east from **Circle, Osu or Accra Central** serve it. I did not find a verified route list. *To be mapped on the recce.*
