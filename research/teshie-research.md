# Teshie: research dossier (draft, November 2026)

**Purpose:** a starting point for the site and the shoot. It is built only from published sources found online. Nothing here should go out as final until it has been checked with the people of Teshie: the Teshie Traditional Council, the Numo Nmashie family, elders and the palace linguist.

**Confidence levels:**
- **High:** several independent sources agree, or it is a well-documented fact (for example, a European fort's date).
- **Medium:** one solid source, or sources broadly agree but differ on details.
- **Low:** a single popular or online source, or the sources conflict. Treat it as a question to ask, not a fact.

## 2. How Teshie began (the founding story)

### What the published accounts say
The Numo Nmashie family tradition, as reported by the Accra Street Journal (April 2026) [2][3]:
- **Ga origins:** two migrating groups joined at Asoye (in present-day Benin). The Bonny group came from Ile-Ife (Nigeria) and the Boma group from the area of present-day DR Congo. They moved via Ladoku (Shai Hills) and the Adjangote Hills, and founded **La** on the coast, around 1600–1629.
- **Leaders at La:** Odoi Atsem led the Bonny group and became **Mantse** (chief) of La. **Numo Nmashie** led the Boma group and became **Mankralo**, the second-in-command, "akin to a prime minister".
- **The split:** around **1710** a feud between the two threatened civil war. The Ga Mashie king tried to mediate, but Nmashie moved east to an area called **Ledzekoku**. He **bought land from the Nungua Mantse, Afote Okley**, and founded Teshie.
- **How the town was set up:** Nmashie did not make himself chief. He appointed **Nii Trebi** as chief and kept the Mankralo role himself. He created three quarters, **Krobo, Agbawe and Klemusum**, and entrusted the **Lakpa** deity to Klemusum.
- **Settlers:** the town drew people from La, Tema, Nungua, Ga Mashie and Adangme Gbugbla (Prampram), plus some Ewe and Akan migrants. Teshie is described as a "settler town par excellence" [4].

Confidence: **Medium** for the broad story (a split from La, land bought from Nungua, founded around 1700). It is repeated by Wikipedia [1] and several popular sources. **Low** for the specific names and the founding of the quarters, since there is essentially one published source for them. These are exactly what to verify on camera.

### Where accounts differ (show these side by side, as the site's rules require)
- **Date:** family tradition says about 1710, based on counting generations. Historian **Wendell Adjetey**, a descendant of Teshie's founders, says the **late 1600s**, and describes Teshie as a "garrison" founded after civil conflict in La [2][3]. Wikipedia says the town was "believed to be 300 years old as of 2011" [1].
- **Quarters:** the founding story names 3 quarters. A district profile says Teshie has **7 quarters**, including Leshie, Krobo, Kle Musum, Agbawe and Gbugbla, with **Leshie** being the quarter the Mantse comes from [6]. The list in that source is cut off. *Ask: what are all seven, and when were the others added?*
- **Land ownership:** the family says the Numo Nmashie family owns Teshie's lands. The 1941 Gold Coast Chief List names both the Numo Nmashie family and the **Dzirasee family of La** [2]. Sensitive: handle with care.
- **Where the first settlers came from:** most sources say La. One local history says some came from "Kpeshie" (variously placed, including in Togo) [8]. Low confidence.

### The name "Teshie"
- One published explanation: **Te** = stone and **Shie** = under or beneath, so "**under the stone**" [4]. A related local story says travellers on the Okangman route described the town by the people "still living beneath the cave" [4].
- Another source says the town was also called by its founder's name [4].
- **Confidence: Low.** This is the "palace linguist" question. Get the Ga spelling and meaning on camera.

### Teshie as a "warrior town"
- A Ga accolade, "**Gonti ni ashiii see anmoo kpo**", is linked to a time when the whole Ga State fought under the Teshie Mantse to repel an attack on Ga Mashie by "Obutu aggressors" [3]. *No translation is published. Ask an elder to translate and explain it, since it could make a great pull quote.*
- Teshie fighters are credited with bravery at the **Battle of Katamanso (1826)**, where Ga forces fought alongside the British and others against Asante. Medium confidence for the battle, Low for Teshie's specific role [2][3].

## 3. What the written record says
- **Fort Augustaborg (1787):** built by the Danes under Governor Jens Adolph Kiøge, named after Princess Louise Augusta, and the last European fort built on the Gold Coast. Denmark sold its forts to Britain (sale agreed in 1849, flag lowered in 1850), and Britain held it until independence in 1957. Today it is **ruins**, part of Ghana's UNESCO-listed forts and castles. **High** confidence [9][10].
- **1941 Gold Coast Chief List:** lists 33 villages under Teshie and 25 under La [2].
- **D.A.E. Gilbert (1966), *Nigerian Geographical Journal*:** describes 24 subsidiary villages behind Teshie toward the Akuapem Ridge [2].
- **1927 Native Administration Ordinance:** made the Ga Mantse president of the Ga State Council. Teshie's chiefs have historically resisted the idea that this subordinated them [3].
- **2011 National House of Chiefs sub-committee:** found that the Teshie, Osu, La, Nungua and Tema stools hold no allegiance to the Ga Paramount Stool [2].
- **Chieftaincy today:** the current Teshie Mantse is **Gbetsoolo Nii Ashitey Akomfra III** (also spelled Okanfra) [11]. For many years before that, Teshie was without a substantive chief (the 2011 reports mention "26 years without a chief") [2]. *Chieftaincy is contested ground. Keep the site neutral and credit the Traditional Council.*

## 4. Texture

### Festival: Homowo in Teshie
- **Homowo** means "hooting at hunger". It marks the Ga triumph over a historic famine and gives thanks for the harvest [12][13].
- **Teshie is the last Ga town to celebrate Homowo, from August to September** [7]. Medium confidence: this comes from the Traditional Council's own site, which was offline when checked.
- **Rites:** maize for **kpokpoi** is sown in May. Before the festival, the Ga Traditional Area observes a **ban on drumming and noise-making** of about a month, with dates set each year (in 2026 it ran 4 May to 4 June). Teshie has been listed as **outside** the Ga Mashie ban in some years [14][15]. On the main day, family heads **sprinkle kpokpoi** (*nishwamo*) and pour libation for the ancestors [12].
- **In Teshie specifically:** traditionally all-white clothing, honouring **Tettey Laakpa**, described as the town's patron deity [11]. There are also roughly **30 days of street music and dance by *kpãshimɔ* groups** [7].
- **Recent context (handle sensitively):** the 2025 Teshie kpokpoi-sprinkling day saw deadly clashes, with reports of 2–3 deaths. The Traditional Council condemned the violence and the Mantse announced a "safer, bigger" Homowo for 2026 [11][16]. *This matters for shoot planning: coordinate with the Council and police if filming during Homowo.*

### Food
- **Kpokpoi (also kpekple):** the Homowo dish of steamed, fermented corn meal eaten with **palm nut soup** and smoked fish. The traditional fish is **tsile** (red sunbeam fish) [12][17]. High confidence.
- No reliable source names a dish *specific* to Teshie beyond Homowo food. *Ask locals: what is Teshie's own dish, and where do they eat it?*

### Craft: fantasy coffins (*abebuu adekai*)
- The Ga name **abebuu adekai** is translated as "**receptacles of proverbs**". Many coffin shapes refer to proverbs or the dead person's work (fish, canoes, tools). Also called *okadi adekai* [5][18].
- **Seth Kane Kwei** (1922–1992) set up a workshop in Teshie in the 1950s. He was long credited with inventing the form, but anthropologist **Regula Tschumi** has argued that other carpenters made similar coffins at about the same time or earlier [5]. High confidence that both views exist.
- **Lineage:** after Kane Kwei, his son Sowah ran the workshop (until 1999), then Cedi Kane Kwei. Today it is run by his grandson **Eric Adjetey Anang** [5][19]. **Paa Joe** apprenticed there and left in 1974 to open his own workshop in Nungua in 1976 [5].
- Fantasy coffins are still used at real funerals, particularly in Teshie, Nungua and La [5].

### Fishing
- Teshie is one of the Ga canoe-landing beaches. Large wooden canoes, now with outboard motors, go out at night and come back in the morning, and are rolled ashore on pipes [20]. Medium confidence (a visitor account).
- **Women's trade:** smoking and drying fish is traditionally women's work along this coast. Sun-drying on the sand has been photographed at **Teshie Nshorna** [21]. Medium confidence.
- **Kpeshie Lagoon and salt:** one academic study says the lagoon once produced abundant salt, until the industry ended during the Second World War. It also mentions a ritual fishing ban on the lagoon called *Gonfi maa* [22]. Low confidence, single source. *A great question for an elder.*

## The other coast towns (for the map and "Next on the coast")
General-reference facts only, to be researched properly before each town's shoot:
- **La (Labadi):** Teshie's parent town in the founding story. It has its own Mantse and Homowo, and Labadi Beach is the busiest beach on the coast [1][2]. It was elevated to paramount status in 2011 [2].
- **Nungua:** Teshie's eastern neighbour. In the published story, the Nungua Mantse sold Teshie's founder his land [3]. Paa Joe's coffin workshop opened there in 1976 [5]. Paramount status since 2011 [2].
- **Osu:** grew up beside Christiansborg Castle, built by the Danes in the 17th century and later the seat of government. Osu was settled on land granted before 1629 (Britannica / Ga histories).
- **Jamestown (Ngleshie Alata):** grew up around James Fort, built by the English in the 1670s, by workers who stayed on after building it (GhanaWeb, "The Definitive Story of James Town"). Known for its lighthouse and its boxing gyms in Bukom.
- **Tema:** an old Ga fishing town. When the harbour was built (opened 1962), the old town was moved to Tema Manhean ("Tema New Town"). *Verify before publishing.*
