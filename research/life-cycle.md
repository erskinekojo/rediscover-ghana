# Ga life-cycle customs: research dossier (draft, November 2026)

**Purpose:** starting facts for Part 2 of the documentary ([../documentary/00-outline.md](../documentary/00-outline.md)): birth and naming, growing up, marriage, funerals. Built only from published sources found online. **Nothing here is final until an elder, a family and the traditional councils confirm it.** Confidence levels as in [teshie-research.md](teshie-research.md): **High**, **Medium**, **Low**.

**Honest summary:** this is **light research.** Searches returned mostly popular and lifestyle pages, with scholarly leads I could not read. Customs vary by town, family and generation, and many have changed (hospital births, shorter ceremonies). Treat everything as a **question to take to the shoot**, not a script.

**Important caution on terminology:** many sources write "Ga-Adangbe" or "Ga-Dangme" and mix practices of the Ga with the neighbouring Dangme (for example Krobo's Dipo puberty rite). **Don't attribute a Dangme practice to the Ga coast** without an elder confirming.

---

## 1. Naming ceremony (outdooring): kpojiemɔ / kpodziemɔ

| Claim | Confidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Ga call it **kpojiemɔ** (also spelled **kpodziemɔ**), literally "out-dooring": the baby is brought out and named | High | Several popular sources agree on the name and meaning [1][2][3] |
| Held on the **eighth day** after birth | High–Medium | Agreed by most sources [1][3][4] |
| Held **early morning / at dawn, before sunrise** | Medium | One source says dawn; another says early morning [2][4] |
| It must fall on the **same day of the week** the child was born | Low | One source says so; another just counts eight days. **Ask an elder** [1][2] |
| Usually at the **paternal grandfather's or father's home** (patrilineal) | Medium | One source [2] |
| Named participants include the **paternal grandfather, two "otsiame", and a godfather or godmother** | Low | One source; the role of *otsiame* is unexplained. **Ask what it means** [2] |
| **Water on the tongue (truth, goodness) and alcohol (falsehood, evil)**; the child is lifted toward the sky and lowered | Medium | Sources agree on water and alcohol but **differ on the order and the symbolism** [2][3]. **Don't state an order: ask** |
| A finger dipped in drink touches the tongue to introduce the child to "the taste of the land" | Low | One source [3] |
| A child's name can combine **day-name, family name, kra name, fetish name, nickname** | Medium | GhanaWeb summary of Ga naming [5]. **Names and meanings: only what a family gives you** |
| Invitation phrase "atsɛbo bɔno", rendered as "you are being called onto the dew" | Low | One search summary; **a spelling and a translation I cannot verify. 🗣 Ask a Ga speaker; do not use unchecked** |
| Today, many naming-related steps happen in hospitals; the ceremony is often symbolic | Medium | [2] |

**Best leads to read next:** the *kpodziemɔ* page on thegadangme.com (by Naa Adjeley Tsofanye, with a linked PDF) [3]; *Ganyobi Kpojiemɔ* by Odotei; Ernest Tetteh's book on the outdooring, dedication and naming of the Ga [6].

## 2. Birth, childhood and coming of age
- Scholarly framing: Ga life is marked by **birth with naming, puberty rites, marriage, and death with funeral rites** [7]. Medium.
- One Ghanaian source says puberty rites, not marriage rites, were traditionally the basis of "legitimate birth" among some Ga sections, and that **La families kept the rites minimal**, with consequences for a girl who became pregnant before them. **Low** (older ethnographic note; sensitive; **do not use without a family's own account**) [8].
- **Dipo is Krobo (Dangme), not Ga**: don't conflate.
- Nothing reliable found on Ga pregnancy customs or childhood. *Ask.*

## 3. Marriage
- Ga marriage under custom is **legally recognised** in Ghana as a full marriage [9]. Medium.
- A lifestyle site calls the knocking ceremony of the Ga-Adangbe **"Agboshimo"**, and describes the groom's family being kept outside until the knocking is done, then a **long engagement list in three parts**, with items such as a suitcase called "Yooshi Bimor" [9]. **Low.** *Single commercial source. The Ga terms and the list need a Ga speaker and a family to confirm before use.*
- Food at the ceremony is described as "Eto" and jollof [9]. Low.
- Frame for the film: *marriage joins two families*; ask who speaks for each side, what is brought, and what is given.

## 4. Funerals
| Claim | Confidence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Every Ga person belongs to a **family house**, which has a special room where the dead is bathed and laid in state; **family elders pour libation** after | Medium | [10] |
| Standard parts: body preparation, formal mourning, drumming, libation, invoking the ancestors | Medium | Scholarly summary [11] |
| The wake runs overnight with burial the next morning, **in one royal funeral** | Low as a rule | [12]: one high-profile case |
| Family elders **set the date and list burial items** | Medium | [10] |
| **Burial inside residential compounds in Accra was prohibited by 1888** (colonial rule); Ga rites changed from the late 19th century | Medium | [13] |
| Bodies can spend **months or years in mortuary storage** while the family organises the funeral | Medium | [14] |
| Funerals involve pre- and post-burial celebrations; a 2023 study covers contemporary "special funeral" rites | Medium | [7] |
| **The ancestral realm is a continuation of this one**, not a sealed-off place | Low | One essay [15] |
| **Fantasy coffins:** 1950s, Teshie, **Seth Kane Kwei**; the family describes the deceased and the maker turns it into a form; revealed at the funeral | High–Medium | [14][16]; see [teshie-research.md](teshie-research.md) |
| Another origin story: a Ga leader ordered a cocoa-pod sculpture, died, and it became his coffin | Low | [14] |
| Some scholars argue that the label "fantasy" **understates** the practice | Low–Medium | [15] |

**Gaps:** I could **not** confirm a standard timeline (one week, final rites), colours or clothing, the ritual words, or who pays: **don't say them until a family explains.** The best lead is the University of Ghana history thesis on Ga funerals in Jamestown [10], and Quarcoopome's "Transformation of Ga death and funeral rites in Accra" (UG) [17].

## 5. What a shoot must ask (first draft)
**Naming:** Who decides the name? Walk through the dawn morning in order. What is said, poured and given? What is the order of water and drink, and what does each mean? What does *otsiame* mean? Does the day-of-the-week rule hold in your family? What changes in town vs. hospital?
**Growing up:** What is done today to mark a child becoming a young adult in your family? (Do not assume a rite.)
**Marriage:** What are the steps and the Ga words for each? Who speaks for each family? What is on the list, and what is its meaning?
**Funeral:** What happens from the moment of death? Who is told, in what order? What are the clothes, colours and drumming? How long does a family wait, and why? What happens after burial?
**All:** What may and may not be filmed? Who must give consent?
