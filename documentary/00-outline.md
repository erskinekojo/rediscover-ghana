# The Ga Coast: the full documentary (outline)

**Working title:** *The Ga Coast* (placeholder). **Target:** about 120 minutes, built as **12 episodes of about 10 minutes** that also play as one film. **Maker:** Kojo / KEMA.

This is a **structure, not a script.** Facts live in [../research/](../research/); the town films in [../voiceover/](../voiceover/) become chapters here.

## The rules (same as the project)
1. Never invent history, names, customs or quotes. Unknowns stay as `[brackets]`.
2. Different versions side by side, never ranked. Oral tradition and written record labelled separately.
3. Contested matters (chieftaincy, land, violence) get every party's voice.
4. **Consent** for every speaker, and from each traditional council before filming rituals, shrines or ceremonies. Ask what must not be filmed.
5. Elders' words replace the narrator wherever possible.

## The pattern for every topic: "shared, then local"
Each subchapter follows three beats, so the film is relatable before it is specific:

| Beat | What it does | Length |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Across the coast** | The pattern most Ga families would recognise | ~1 min |
| **B. Town by town** | Teshie · La · Nungua, differences side by side | ~1 min |
| **C. One family** | A named, consenting family or person, on camera | ~1–2 min |

Every subchapter also ends with a **"Say it" beat** (10–20 seconds): one phrase, said by a local, with Ga, phonetic spelling and English on screen.

## Production notes
- **Shoot once, cut many:** each subchapter (2–4 min) also works as a vertical short for the app.
- **Spoken Ga on screen:** the phrase appears in Ga, in a plain phonetic spelling, and in English. Audio from a native speaker or elder, not the narrator.
- **A recurring family per town:** 2–3 consenting families who appear across Parts 2 to 4, so viewers follow the same people.
- **Pronunciation pass** before recording, using each elder's own pronunciation.
- **Offer a subtitled English edition** and consider a Ga-language edition.
